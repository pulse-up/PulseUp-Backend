# How It Works

> A plain-language guide to PulseUp and an enterprise marketing brief for presenting it as a potential university healthcare system-management platform for South African higher education.

## 1. What PulseUp Is

PulseUp is a student-centred campus healthcare platform connecting three groups in one organised service:

- **Students** book and manage campus clinic visits.
- **Clinic staff** publish availability, review requests, confirm appointments, and complete attended visits.
- **Administrators** support accounts, appointment records, and clinic availability.

The long-term vision is to develop PulseUp from a focused campus-clinic application into a configurable digital operating layer for higher-education healthcare services across South Africa.

PulseUp is not an emergency-care service and should not be presented as a replacement for clinical judgement, emergency channels, or university health policies.

## 2. What Students Can Do

Students can create an account, sign in, select a clinic service, book an available time, add an optional clinic note, and manage eligible appointments. After staff confirmation, they can view the clinician, clinic room, calculated queue position, and estimated wait. After an eligible completed visit, they receive 10 Health Points.

## 3. What Clinic Staff Can Do

Clinic staff publish service-linked availability, including the date, time, room, and expected duration. They review student requests, confirm Pending or Rescheduled appointments, and mark attended visits as Completed.

## 4. What Administrators Can Do

Administrators support user accounts, appointment records, and clinic availability. They can create staff and administrator accounts, review system records, and manage eligible time slots and appointments.

## 5. How Booking Works

1. **Staff publishes availability.**
2. **Student selects a service and available time.**
3. **The booking becomes Pending.**
4. **Staff confirms the request.**
5. **The student sees visit and calculated queue information.**
6. **Staff completes the attended visit.**
7. **The student receives 10 Health Points.**

## 6. Appointment Statuses

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Pending** | The request is waiting for staff confirmation. |
| **Confirmed** | Staff accepted the booking and visit information is available. |
| **Rescheduled** | The student selected another matching available time. |
| **Cancelled** | The appointment will not take place and the old time is released. |
| **Completed** | The visit took place and Health Points are awarded. |

## 7. Queue and Reward Accuracy

PulseUp can display calculated queue position, estimated wait, clinician, room, date, and time for a confirmed appointment. It does not currently provide automatic push notifications, physical check-in, a “now serving” console, or guaranteed waiting times.

The visible rewards experience focuses on an R100 Cyngatha food-voucher goal at 100 points. The current student interface does not yet expose a complete redemption journey. Reward eligibility, availability, values, expiry, and redemption terms apply.

## 8. Current Product Boundaries

Do not claim that the current implementation already provides:

- Automatic live push notifications.
- Guaranteed waiting times.
- Emergency treatment.
- Full electronic health records.
- Completed enterprise integrations or national adoption.
- Enterprise certification or confirmed POPIA compliance.
- Instant voucher redemption.

These may be evaluated as future roadmap items, subject to product, clinical, legal, security, and institutional review.

## 13. Minimal Enterprise Design System

### Product-first visual policy

The deck must be **product-first, not people-first**.

- Use real PulseUp UI crops, diagrams, typography, line icons, structured cards, and whitespace as the primary visual language.
- Do not generate groups of people for the slides.
- Do not use AI-generated human illustrations; they often appear cartoon-like and weaken enterprise credibility.
- Human imagery is optional on **Slide 3 only**.
- If Slide 3 uses a person, choose one professionally licensed, high-resolution editorial photograph of an adult South African university student with natural lighting and an uncluttered background.
- If a suitable photograph is unavailable, use a minimal abstract silhouette or omit the person entirely.
- All other slides must use no people imagery.

### UX and UI laws applied to slides

1. **Hick’s Law:** give the viewer one primary message, not many competing choices.
2. **Miller’s Law:** group information into three or four memorable chunks; use five only for a necessary process.
3. **Gestalt Proximity:** keep related labels and visuals close together.
4. **Common Region:** place related facts inside one card or defined area rather than scattering them.
5. **Similarity and Consistency:** repeat the same card radius, icon weight, spacing, labels, and status treatments.
6. **Von Restorff Effect:** use orange for one standout item only—usually the CTA, key number, or connector.
7. **Fitts’s Law:** make the final CTA and QR target large, isolated, and easy to locate.
8. **Serial Position Effect:** place the most important message first and the action or conclusion last.
9. **Visual Hierarchy:** headline first, hero visual second, supporting evidence third, footnote last.
10. **Accessibility:** maintain at least 4.5:1 contrast for normal text and 3:1 for large text.

### Content-density limits

These are mandatory:

- One headline per slide, ideally 6–10 words.
- One supporting sentence, maximum 24 words.
- One hero visual or diagram.
- Maximum four supporting groups; use five only for the Slide 4 process.
- Maximum 35–45 visible words, excluding short labels, technology names, and legal footnotes.
- At least 35% of every slide must remain open negative space.
- No paragraph blocks on slides; move explanation into speaker notes.
- No more than two card styles on one slide.
- No more than one CTA.
- No decorative element without a communication purpose.

### Spacing and layout

- Use an 8-point spacing system.
- Use a 12-column grid for 16:9, six columns for 4:5, and four columns for 1:1 and 9:16.
- Keep a consistent 24–32 px card radius and 1 px `#DCE6ED` border.
- Use soft shadows below 12% opacity.
- Avoid glassmorphism, heavy gradients, 3D icons, clip art, excessive pills, and crowded dashboards.

### Typography

- **Headlines:** Manrope ExtraBold, 56–72 px on 1920 × 1080.
- **Section headings:** Manrope Bold, 42–56 px.
- **Body text:** DM Sans Regular or Medium, 26–32 px.
- **Labels:** DM Sans Bold, 20–24 px, uppercase.
- **Footnotes:** never below 18 px.
- Fallback: Montserrat and Open Sans.

## 14. Crystal-Clear Product Screens

Whenever UI appears inside a phone, tablet, or laptop:

1. Build the device and interface as separate Canva layers.
2. Insert a high-resolution approved PulseUp screen, ideally exported at 2×.
3. Mask the UI precisely to the device corners.
4. Keep it at 100% opacity with no blur, grain, tint, or glare over text.

Mandatory visibility rules:

- Device tilt: 0–8 degrees from front-facing.
- Important phone display: at least 30% of a 16:9 canvas width.
- Held phone: approximately 650–780 px tall on 1920 × 1080.
- Visible phone screen: at least 320 px wide on the 16:9 master.
- Portrait formats: phone should occupy 45–65% of canvas width.
- No finger, sleeve, reflection, icon, caption, or decoration may cover the UI.
- Use one focused UI module per screen; never squeeze an entire desktop page into a phone.
- Show no more than five meaningful interface elements.
- Required device labels must remain at least 24 px on the final 16:9 export.
- If the screen cannot be read at normal view, enlarge or simplify it. Never accept “almost readable.”

## 15. Multi-Format System

Build the master at **1920 × 1080 (16:9)** and reflow—not crop—the same components into:

| Format | Canvas | Safe zone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Widescreen | 1920 × 1080, 16:9 | 96 px sides; 72 px top and bottom |
| Portrait feed | 1080 × 1350, 4:5 | 72 px on every edge |
| Square | 1080 × 1080, 1:1 | 64 px on every edge |
| Vertical | 1080 × 1920, 9:16 | 72 px sides; 160 px top; 220 px bottom |

Format behaviour:

- **16:9:** use a controlled two-column or centred-diagram layout.
- **4:5:** place headline first, hero visual second, and supporting groups last.
- **1:1:** retain one message and one visual; shorten copy by 25–35%.
- **9:16:** stack headline, visual, and CTA; move secondary detail into the caption.
- Keep complete devices, wordmarks, disclaimers, and CTAs inside safe zones.
